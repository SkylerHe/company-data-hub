## My investing stance (as of 2026-07)
- **Belief:** markets are **mostly efficient, not perfectly** — and can be misleading / manipulated (especially small, illiquid, newly-public names).
- **Therefore:** a **value investor with an index core** — *core-satellite*:
  - 🟢 **Core = broad index (SPY)** — hard to manipulate, captures the market reliably, needs no stock analysis.
  - 🔵 **Satellite = a small active value sleeve** — hunt mispricing in the messy corners, *with fundamentals*, and let the **journal (evidence) decide** if my judgment actually has edge before scaling it.
- Temperament fits value (patient, contrarian, enjoys reading). Belief (efficiency) demands the index base. Evidence gets the final vote.

## The schools of investing (map)
| School | Core idea | Key names |
|---|---|---|
| **Value** (margin of safety) | buy below intrinsic value | Graham, Buffett, Klarman |
| **Growth** | pay up for fast growth | Fisher, Lynch |
| **Index / passive** | own the market cheaply | Bogle, Fama |
| **Quant / factor** | systematic factor tilts | Fama–French, AQR |
| **Momentum / trend** | ride winners | Jegadeesh–Titman |
| **Macro** | bet big economic shifts | Soros, Dalio |
Axes that separate them: *fundamentals vs price vs macro · active vs passive · bottom-up vs top-down · discretionary vs systematic · long vs short horizon.*
Value ⚔️ Momentum and Active ⚔️ Passive genuinely conflict — resolved by keeping **two books**.

## Margin of safety (the core value tool)
- **Intrinsic value** = what a business is truly worth (from earnings/cash flow/assets), separate from price.
- **Margin of safety** = buy well *below* intrinsic value; the discount is the cushion for being wrong.
- It exists **because** valuation is unreliable — it's insurance against my own errors. (Graham / Klarman)
- SPCX was the anti-example: hype price, no discount, no cushion → speculation.

## Why fundamentals? (conditional)
- **Pick individual stocks → fundamentals are essential** (only way to estimate value → margin of safety). Without them, stock-picking = gambling.
- **Index → don't need fundamentals** (the market does the analysis).
- Philosophy = defense (don't lose); fundamentals = offense (a reason to win, only if picking).

## Philosophy vs. fundamentals — reliability
Most → least reliable: **sound risk principles** > audited *past* fundamentals > my valuation > *forecast* fundamentals > price predictions. Above all: **evidence (reality/journal) wins.** Fundamentals *estimate*; philosophy keeps you alive when the estimate is wrong.

## Established evidence I trust (Tier-1, sourced)
- **Costs are the #1 predictor of net returns** (Morningstar; Bogle).
- **Most active managers underperform the index after costs; winners rarely persist** (SPIVA; Sharpe 1991).
- **Diversification cuts *uncompensated* risk without cutting return — if I have no proven edge** (Markowitz/CAPM; conditional per Buffett).
- **Overtrading lowers returns** (Barber & Odean 2000); the **behavior gap** is real (Morningstar).
- **Stocks beat bonds/cash over long horizons** (Dimson–Marsh–Staunton).
- **IPOs underperform on average afterward** (Ritter) — the documented pattern SPCX walked into.
