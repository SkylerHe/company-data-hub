# Trading & Investing Philosophy

**Purpose:** my curated collection of principles I agree with. Claude consults this **before giving any trading/investing suggestion** and grounds recommendations in the relevant principle here (alongside real evidence). I curate this freely — add, edit, or remove as my thinking sharpens.

> **Evidence always wins.** This is a *reference for training a sound investor mindset* — not a rulebook. When a principle conflicts with the evidence, the evidence decides and the principle gets revisited. The goal isn't to obey these; it's to *become* the kind of investor who holds them because they're true.

---

## I. Risk & capital preservation (comes first)
1. **Decide the loss before the entry.** Set max risk *before* caring about upside — the loss is step 1. *(I control the loss, not the win; survival is what lets compounding happen.)*
2. **Never hold a position without defined risk** (a stop) — the one exception is a *deliberate* long-term hold. Undefined downside is the account-killer.
3. **Position size is math, not confidence.** `shares = risk ÷ stop distance`, capped (≤ ~1R/trade, ≤ ~20% per name, ≤ ~5R total). Never sized by gut feeling or "it's simulated."
4. **Small, uniform losses.** Every loss ≈ 1R. One oversized loss can erase many good wins.

## II. Evidence over inference
5. **Recommendations must trace to real evidence** — actual data, real price levels, dated facts — never a rule of thumb dressed up as a level. *(see [[evidence-based-suggestions]])*
6. **Wait for confirmation; don't catch a falling knife.** Let the market *show* me before I act.

## III. Trade management
7. **Never widen a stop — tighten only.** (Correcting an *over-tight* stop while calm and safely above it is OK; moving a stop away to escape a loss is not.)
8. **Protect profit.** Move stops up as price makes higher lows — profit isn't mine until it's protected.
9. **Manage winners, not losers.** Don't "help" a loser by moving its stop further away.

## IV. Investing & valuation mindset
10. **Margin of safety** — buy below intrinsic value; the discount *is* the cushion. Price is what makes a good company safe or dangerous. *(Graham / Klarman)*
11. **Don't overpay for hype.** Buying just because it's rising — no discount, no cushion — is speculation. *(the SPCX lesson)*
12. **Don't market-time a long-term hold.** Time in the market beats timing it; ride the dips.
13. **Diversification cuts *uncompensated* (single-company) risk without lowering expected return — *when* I combine assets of similar expected return.** *(Markowitz / CAPM — evidence-backed, but **conditional**.)*
    - It's a true "free lunch" only when I have **no proven edge**. With a *genuine, demonstrated* edge, concentration can raise expected return at higher risk *(Buffett / Munger)*.
    - "Diversification is protection against ignorance." Even **Buffett wills his estate ~90% into a low-cost S&P 500 index** — he concentrates *his* money (proven edge) and tells everyone else to index.
    - **Where I stand:** no demonstrated edge yet (journal has ~1 closed trade) → **I diversify (index core).** Concentration without edge = uncompensated risk + an unproven bet on my own skill. *(That's the SPCX failure mode.)*
14. **Don't turn a losing trade into a "long-term hold."** Hope is not a thesis. Reclassifying to avoid selling is a trap.

## V. Process & psychology
15. **Two books, kept separate:** long-term **Investments** (hold, no stop) vs. active **Trades** (bracketed).
16. **Judge process, not outcome.** A win with a broken process is still a bad trade; a planned loss is a success.
17. **Journal everything; measure expectancy over ~20+ trades.** Any short streak is noise.
18. **Independent and patient.** Cash while waiting is fine; don't get herded by fear or greed.
