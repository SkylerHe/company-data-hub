## Concept maps to memorize (fixes most misses)

**1. Annual-report components — who provides what** *(Q8, 11, 16, 25 — my weakest area)*
- **Financial statements** — the numbers (income, balance sheet, cash flow, equity).
- **Notes / footnotes** — detail *behind* the numbers (policies, breakdowns, debt terms).
- **MD&A (management commentary)** — management's **narrative**: objectives, strategy, **liquidity, capital resources, off-balance-sheet, trends, risks**. The richest forward-looking source.
- **Auditor's report** — independent opinion that the numbers are fairly stated.

**2. The 4 audit opinions (best → worst)** *(Q12, 15, 29 — 0-for-2, drill this)*
- **Unqualified** = "all clean" ✅ — fairly presented (the *preferred* one).
- **Qualified** = "clean **except for** one issue" ⚠️ — a single scope limit/exception.
- **Adverse** = "these are **WRONG**" 🚩 — *materially misstated, not fairly presented* (auditor **gives** a negative opinion).
- **Disclaimer** = "we **can't say**" 🚫 — auditor is *unable to form* an opinion (severe scope limit / no evidence).
- Two traps: **"qualified" sounds good but isn't** (Q12); **adverse vs disclaimer** — adverse = *concluded wrong*, disclaimer = *couldn't conclude* (Q29).

**3. Regulators vs standard-setters** *(Q22, 17)*
- Regulate the *markets* → **IOSCO** (global coordinator), **SEC** (US).
- Write the *accounting rulebook* → **IASB** (IFRS), **FASB** (US GAAP); *convergence* = aligning them.

**4. Financial analysis framework (6 steps)** *(Q24)*
Purpose → **Collect** → **Process** (ratios, common-size) → **Analyze/interpret** → Conclude/report → Follow-up.

**5. Elements of financial statements — IFRS** *(R2·1)*
- Balance sheet: **Assets · Liabilities · Equity** (Equity = Assets − Liabilities).
- Performance: **Income** (= revenue + gains) · **Expenses**.
- **Income** = equity **↑** from asset **↑** *or* liability **↓** — **EXCEPT owner contributions**.
- **Expenses** = equity **↓** from asset **↓** *or* liability **↑** — **EXCEPT distributions to owners (dividends)**.
- Owner transactions (contributions in / dividends out) are **never** income or expense.

**6. Income-statement top lines** *(R2·2)*
```
Gross revenue
−  Returns & allowances (and discounts)
=  NET REVENUE            ← "net revenue / net sales"
−  Cost of goods sold (COGS)
=  Gross profit
```
Accrual basis → recognize revenue when **earned**, not when **cash** is collected (cash timing is a distractor).

**7. Capitalize vs expense — earnings-quality lever** *(R2·4)*
- **Expense** → full cost hits the income statement **now** → profit **LOWER** now.
- **Capitalize** → cost becomes a balance-sheet **asset**, amortized over future years → profit **HIGHER** now.
- Switching expense→capitalize (e.g. IFRS 15 contract-acquisition costs) → profit appears **higher initially**.
- **Cash flow is unchanged** — only the *timing of the expense* moves; the cash already went out. (profit ≠ cash)

**8. Income-statement presentation & comprehensive income** *(R2·5)*
- **NET INCOME = continuing operations + discontinued operations** (2 sections):
  - **Continuing operations** — ongoing business + **one-off gains/losses** (fire, lawsuit) from businesses you're *keeping*. Main P&L (has many line items).
  - **Discontinued operations** — a division you **decided to dispose of**; separate, **net of tax**.
- **COMPREHENSIVE INCOME = net income + OCI.** OCI is *outside* net income:
  - **Other comprehensive income (OCI)** — items that **bypass net income** → equity (FX translation · pension adj. · FVOCI securities · cash-flow hedges).
- Rule: *disposing a division?* → discontinued · *unrealized/equity item?* → OCI · *else* → continuing.
- **"Extraordinary items" are abolished** (IFRS never; US GAAP dropped 2015) — rare events still go in **continuing operations**.

**9. Accounting changes — the 3-way split** *(R2·6)*
- **Change in POLICY** (a whole method: FIFO→wtd-avg, revenue-rec approach) → **RETROSPECTIVE** → *restate the **prior periods shown in the report*** as if the new policy always applied → keeps the presented years comparable. **Notes** describe the change and justify it.
- **Change in ESTIMATE** (a judgment input: useful life, salvage value, bad-debt %) → **PROSPECTIVE** → **current & future** periods only; **no restatement**. ← this is the "current and future" trap answer.
- **Correction of an ERROR** (a past mistake) → **RETROSPECTIVE RESTATEMENT** of affected prior statements.
- Two traps: (a) B "current & future" *sounds* like the policy answer but is the **estimate** rule; (b) A "all prior periods" overreaches — it's only the periods **shown in the report**, not all history.
- Hook: *Policy → redo the past shown · Estimate → only forward · Error → restate.*

**10. Basic EPS** *(R2·7)*
```
                Net income  −  Preferred dividends
Basic EPS =  ───────────────────────────────────────
              Weighted-average common shares outstanding
```
- **Numerator:** subtract **preferred** dividends **only**. **Common** dividends are *never* subtracted (they're a distribution of the earnings, not a claim ahead of common).
- **Denominator:** weight each issuance/buyback by the **fraction of the year outstanding**. Shares issued mid-year (Jul 1) count for 6/12: 100,000 × 0.5 = **50,000** → total **1,050,000**.
- **Trap B (0.91):** counted the full 100,000 new shares (1,000,000 ÷ 1,100,000) — forgot to weight for half-year.
- **Trap A (0.80):** wrongly subtracted the $200,000 **common** dividend *and* ignored the new shares.
- **Distractor:** the **$20 issue price** is irrelevant to EPS (it's cash raised, not a share count).
- Weighted-avg shortcut: `Σ(shares × months outstanding) ÷ 12`.
- Hook: *Preferred out of the top · weight the bottom by time.*
- **Basic EPS always uses WEIGHTED average shares — there is no "simple average" version.** "Simple average" on an exam is *always* the wrong choice. *(R2·8)*

**11. Simple vs complex capital structure** *(R2·8)*
- **Complex** = the company has **potentially dilutive securities** — convertible bonds, convertible preferred, **options, warrants**. → must report **both basic AND diluted EPS**.
- **Simple** = **none** of those. → report **basic only**, and **basic EPS = diluted EPS** (nothing to dilute).
- **Issuing common stock several times does NOT make a structure complex** — plain common shares aren't dilutive; they just change the *weighting* in the denominator (that's the Basic-EPS job, map #10). Only *convertible/optionable* instruments trigger "complex."
- Hook: *Complex = something can turn INTO shares. No convertibles/options → simple → basic = diluted.*

**12. Diluted EPS — the 3 methods** *(R2·9)* — *"if every dilutive security converted, what's EPS?" Always ≤ basic.*
```
             (NI − Pref divs) + add-backs
Diluted = ─────────────────────────────────────
          Wtd-avg shares + new shares from conversion
```
| Dilutive security | Numerator add-back | Denominator + |
|---|---|---|
| **Convertible preferred** | + preferred dividend (**no tax**) → cancels the subtraction → numerator = **plain NI** | + shares from conversion (pref × conversion ratio) |
| **Convertible bond** | + interest **× (1 − tax rate)** | + shares from conversion |
| **Options / warrants** | none | + **net** new shares (treasury-stock method) |
- **Convertible preferred (R2·9):** the div you subtracted for *basic* gets **added back** → numerator becomes plain net income. Only the denominator grows. `200M ÷ (50M + 2M×2) = 3.70`.
- **Convertible bond twist:** interest is added back **after tax** (interest is tax-deductible; preferred dividends are not).
- **Options/warrants (treasury method):** net shares = `options × (mkt − strike)/mkt`; only dilutive when **in the money** (mkt > strike).
- **Antidilutive rule:** if including a security would *raise* EPS, **exclude it** — diluted can never exceed basic.
- Common error (Q9 trap A): converting shares in the denominator but forgetting to add the dividend back in the numerator → inconsistent. *Convert = both moves or neither.*
- Common error (Q10 trap A): adding the **full** option count instead of **net** shares — forgot the treasury-stock buyback. Options add net dilution only.
- Common error (Q10 trap C): forgetting to subtract the preferred dividend when the preferred is **not** the converting security.
- Hook: *Add back what you'd stop paying · add the shares you'd issue · drop anything that helps EPS.*
- **Reverse-framing the exam loves (R2·13):** "which security raises shares **without** affecting net-income-to-common?" → **stock options** (denominator-only, no add-back). Convertible debt/preferred both change the numerator (interest / dividend add-back). *Options add back nothing.*

**⚠️ The Q9 ↔ Q10 preferred-dividend trap (memorize the discriminator)**
The key question: **is the *preferred stock itself* the thing converting?**
| Scenario | Preferred dividend in numerator | Denominator adds |
|---|---|---|
| **Convertible preferred converts** (R2·9) | **Added back → cancels** → numerator = plain NI | full converted shares (pref × ratio) |
| **Options/bonds dilute, plain preferred present** (R2·10) | **Stays subtracted** (preferred isn't converting) | net option shares / bond-converted shares |
- Yes, the preferred converts → dividend disappears. No, something else dilutes → preferred dividend stays subtracted. This is the single most common diluted-EPS trap.

**Capstone: MULTIPLE convertibles at once (R2·16)** — when a company has convertible preferred AND convertible bonds:
1. **Basic EPS** = (NI − pref div) / common shares.
2. **Test each convertible for dilution** individually: incremental EPS = add-back ÷ new shares. If < basic EPS → dilutive (include); if > → antidilutive (exclude).
3. **Include all dilutive ones together**: numerator = NI − pref div + (pref div added back) + (after-tax bond interest); denominator = common + all converted shares.
- Preferred add-back = the dividend (no tax); bond add-back = interest × (1 − tax). *Both* their shares go in the denominator.
- Rank most-dilutive-first and add one at a time; keep any that keeps EPS falling. (In practice, if both individually dilute below basic, both are in.)

## Key terms glossary (plain-term)
- **Notes (footnotes)** — disclosures explaining the numbers: policies, breakdowns, debt terms. *(Q8, 25)*
- **Management commentary / MD&A** — management's narrative: objectives, strategy, liquidity, capital resources, off-balance-sheet, trends, risks. *(Q11, 16, 25)*
- **Auditor's report** — independent firm's opinion on fair presentation. *(Q11, 12, 16)*
- **Unqualified / qualified / adverse / disclaimer** — clean ▸ except-for ▸ *misstated (opinion given)* ▸ *can't-opine (no opinion)*. *(Q12, 29)*
- **Adverse vs disclaimer** — adverse = auditor **concluded** the statements are wrong; disclaimer = auditor **couldn't conclude** at all. *(Q29)*
- **Financial reporting** — companies **providing information** about performance, financial position, and changes in position. *(Q28)*
- **Financial statement analysis** — **using** those reports (+ other info) to **evaluate** and make investment/credit/economic decisions. *(Q28)*
- **Performance / financial position / changes** — income statement / balance sheet / cash-flow-over-time, respectively. *(Q28)*
- **Unaudited** — not examined by an auditor; interim (quarterly) reports are unaudited. ≠ *unqualified*. *(Q15)*
- **Reasonable assurance** — high-but-not-absolute confidence; auditors sample, can't catch everything. *(Q17)*
- **Materiality** — big enough to change a user's decision; auditors focus here. *(Q17)*
- **Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX)** — 2002 US law (post-Enron); requires an opinion on internal controls. *(Q17)*
- **Internal controls / material weakness** — company's accuracy safeguards; a disclosed weakness = accounting red flag. *(Q17)*
- **PCAOB** — sets US public-company audit standards. *(Q17)*
- **GAAP / IFRS** — US / international accounting rulebooks. *(Q17, 22)*
- **IOSCO** — global coordinator of securities *regulators* (uniform regulation + cross-border cooperation); not a standard-setter. *(Q22)*
- **SEC** — US securities *regulator*; IOSCO member. *(Q22)*
- **IASB / FASB** — accounting *standard-setters* (IFRS / US GAAP); *convergence* = aligning them. *(Q22)*
- **Common-size statements** — line items as %; built in the *process* step. **Income statement → % of REVENUE; balance sheet → % of TOTAL ASSETS** (never net income). Removes company-size effect → enables cross-sectional (across companies) + time-series comparison, and reveals strategy/cost-structure differences. *(Q24, R2·14)*
- **Capital resources** — sources of funding (debt, equity, off-balance-sheet). *(Q25)*
- **Off-balance-sheet financing** — funding not shown as debt on the balance sheet; can hide leverage (Enron). *(Q25)*
- **Contractual commitments / purchase obligations** — committed future cash outflows not yet on the balance sheet. *(Q25)*
- **Liquidity** — ability to meet short-term obligations / access cash. *(Q25)*
- **Income (IFRS)** — increases in economic benefits (asset ↑ or liability ↓) that raise equity, **excluding owner contributions**; = revenue + gains. *(R2·1)*
- **Enhancements of assets** — assets increasing (inflows or gains in value). *(R2·1)*
- **Owners' contributions** — capital owners inject (buying shares); raises equity but is **not** income. *(R2·1)*
- **Accrual vs cash basis** — accrual = recognize revenue/expense when **earned/incurred** (GAAP/IFRS); cash = when **cash received/paid**. *(R2·2)*
- **Net revenue** — gross revenue **− returns & allowances** (− discounts). Not the same as gross profit (that's after COGS). *(R2·2)*
- **Returns & allowances** — goods customers return / price reductions granted; reduce revenue. *(R2·2)*
- **Capitalize vs expense** — expense = full cost to income statement now (profit ↓); capitalize = record as asset, amortize later (profit ↑ now, **cash unchanged**). *(R2·4)*
- **Incremental costs of obtaining a contract** — costs incurred *only because* you won the deal (e.g., sales commission); **capitalized** under IFRS 15/ASC 606. *(R2·4)*
- **IFRS 15 / ASC 606** — the May 2014 IASB+FASB **converged revenue-recognition** standard; requires capitalizing contract-acquisition costs. *(R2·4)*
- **Continuing operations** — ongoing-business results, incl. infrequent one-off gains/losses (fire, lawsuit); the main income statement. *(R2·5)*
- **Discontinued operations** — a component the company **decided to sell/shut down**; reported separately, net of tax. *(R2·5)*
- **Other comprehensive income (OCI)** — gains/losses that **bypass net income** → equity: FX translation, pension adj., FVOCI securities, cash-flow hedges. *(R2·5)*
- **Extraordinary items** — **abolished** category (IFRS never allowed; US GAAP removed 2015); no special bucket for rare events. *(R2·5)*
- **Change in accounting policy** — switching the whole method (e.g. FIFO→weighted-average); applied **retrospectively** → restate prior periods shown. *(R2·6)*
- **Retrospective application / restatement** — redo the prior periods *shown in the report* as if the new policy had always been used → comparability across the presented years. *(R2·6)*
- **Change in accounting estimate** — revising a judgment input (useful life, salvage, bad-debt %); applied **prospectively** (current & future only), **no restatement**. *(R2·6)*
- **Prospective application** — apply the change going forward only; leave prior reported periods untouched. *(R2·6)*
- **Basic EPS** — (net income − preferred dividends) ÷ weighted-average common shares outstanding. *(R2·7)*
- **Weighted-average shares outstanding** — share count weighted by the fraction of the period each block was outstanding; mid-year issues/buybacks count only for their months. *(R2·7)*
- **Preferred vs common dividends (in EPS)** — subtract **preferred** dividends from the numerator; **common** dividends are **never** subtracted. *(R2·7)*
- **Simple capital structure** — no potentially dilutive securities; company reports **basic EPS only**, and basic = diluted. *(R2·8)*
- **Complex capital structure** — has potentially dilutive securities (convertibles, options, warrants); must report **both basic and diluted EPS**. *(R2·8)*
- **Potentially dilutive securities** — instruments that could convert into common shares (convertible bonds/preferred, options, warrants) and lower EPS. *(R2·8)*
- **Diluted EPS** — EPS assuming all dilutive securities convert; always ≤ basic EPS. *(R2·9)*
- **If-converted method** — for convertibles: assume conversion at period start; add back the dividend/after-tax interest to the numerator and the new shares to the denominator. *(R2·9)*
- **Treasury-stock method** — for options/warrants: assume exercise proceeds buy back shares at market; add only the *net* new shares = `options × (mkt − strike)/mkt`. *(R2·9, R2·10)*
- **Net new shares (options)** — shares issued at exercise minus shares repurchased with the proceeds; the only dilution options add. *(R2·10)*
- **In the money** — market price > exercise/strike price; options only dilute (only get included) when in the money. *(R2·10)*
- **Antidilutive security** — one whose inclusion would *raise* EPS; excluded from diluted EPS. *(R2·9)*

## Key facts to memorize (from CFA answer feedback)
Authoritative statements pulled straight from the official feedback — memorize these
close to verbatim. *(Includes a few from questions not individually logged above.)*

**Financial analysis framework & purpose**
- Analysts examine a company's **past and current** performance & financial position to form **expectations about its future** performance & position.
- **Follow-up phase** = gather information and **repeat the analysis** to determine whether reports/recommendations need updating. *(the 6th/last framework step)*

**Reporting vs analysis** *(the Q28 pair — one *provides*, one *uses*)*
- **Role of financial *reporting*** = provide information about a company's **performance, financial position, and changes in financial position**, useful to a **wide range of users** making economic decisions.
- **Role of financial statement *analysis*** = **use** those financial reports to evaluate a company's **past, current, and potential** performance & position, for **investment, credit, and other economic decisions**.

**Standard-setters**
- **IASB** develops **IFRS** (International Financial Reporting Standards).
- **FASB** develops **US GAAP**.

**IOSCO**
- Assists in **uniform regulation** + **cross-border cooperation** in combating violations of securities & derivatives laws.
- **3 core objectives:** (1) markets are **fair, efficient, and transparent**; (2) **reduce** (not *eliminate*) **systemic risk**; (3) **protect investors** (not *all users* of financial statements).

**Annual-report components**
- The **notes** provide information **essential to understanding** the primary statements.
- **Objectives, strategies, and significant risks** → **management commentary (MD&A)**.

**Audit opinions**
- **Unqualified** ("clean") = statements present performance & position **fairly** in accordance with accounting standards.
- **Qualified** = some **scope limitation or exception** to standards; described in **explanatory paragraphs** so the analyst can judge the exception's importance.

**Interim reporting**
- Interim reports are **semiannual or quarterly**, include **unaudited** financial statements + an **MD&A**. ("Unqualified" is a **type of audit opinion**, not a reporting frequency/status.)

**Regulatory nuance (easy to miss)**
- The **annual report is NOT a requirement of the US SEC.** *(The SEC requires the 10-K; the glossy "annual report to shareholders" itself is not SEC-mandated.)*
